# Mythology Script Writing (Hindi VO, cross-epic style)

This skill reproduces the writing method of the reference video
("Prequel: Hanuman in Patala" style, ~13 min, kathputli / 2.5D shadow-theatre
visuals, Hindi voiceover with English subtitles). Full beat-by-beat breakdown
of that video: `references/reference-breakdown.md`. Output format:
`references/script-template.md`. Craft devices with examples:
`references/techniques.md`.

The core idea of the reference: **a single thread character carries the
audience from one epic into another** (Hanuman: Ramayana → Mahabharata), and
every Dwapar event is shown as an echo of a Treta event — so the viewer
"discovers" that the two stories are one story. The ending turns that echo
into a philosophical point (dharma declines yug by yug).

## Workflow

1. **Lock the brief** (ask only what is missing; otherwise pick defaults):
   - Topic / thread character (default: a chiranjeevi — Hanuman, Parashuram,
     Vibhishan, Jambavan, Ashwatthama, Markandeya, Vyasa).
   - Length: reel (60-90 s, ~150-220 words) or long-form (8-15 min,
     ~1200-2000 Hindi words; narration speed ≈ 140-150 words/min).
   - Series context: is there a prequel/sequel reel to call back to?
2. **Research from primary texts first.** Use Valmiki Ramayana, Vyasa
   Mahabharata (incl. Vana Parva, Ramopakhyana, Bhishma Parva/Gita),
   Puranas. Note for every major event: parva/kanda, and 1-3 shlokas worth
   quoting. Mark anything that is folk / regional / later tradition as such
   — never present a folk version as "Valmiki ne likha".
3. **Find the spine** — write these 3 lines before anything else:
   - *Thread*: who/what carries us across the story (character, boon, object, vow).
   - *Echo pairs*: 3-5 pairs "Treta mein X ↔ Dwapar mein Y"
     (e.g. Vibhishan leaves Lanka ↔ Yuyutsu leaves Kaurav army).
   - *Closing truth*: one sentence the whole film earns
     (e.g. "Treta mein ye shabd ek rakshas ke the, Dwapar mein ek veer ki daleel — wo yug ab nahi raha.").
4. **Build the beat sheet** using the 9-act structure below. Give each beat a
   timestamp budget.
5. **Write the VO** in the template format (VO line + visual cue + English
   subtitle). Apply the language rules.
6. **Self-check** with the checklist at the end; fix, then deliver.

## The 9-act structure (from the reference)

| # | Act | Share | What happens |
|---|-----|-------|--------------|
| 1 | Series hook | 0-3% | Callback to previous reel ("wo kahani hamari ek reel mein hai, link upar / pinned"). One line, then straight into story. |
| 2 | Cold-open image | 3-6% | A quiet, emotional tableau after a big event (empty Sarayu ghat steps, only flowers on water — Shri Ram has gone). Then the twist: "par Hanuman ko jaana nahi tha." |
| 3 | The vow / boon | 6-8% | Flashback to the promise that drives the thread. Quote the Sanskrit shloka, then the plain Hindi meaning, then the answer ("aur Shri Ram ne kaha tha — tathastu."). |
| 4 | Yug transition + roll call | 8-15% | "Treta yug beeta, yug ka pahiya ghooma…" Montage: where every surviving character is now (Vibhishan in Lanka, Jambavan in his cave, Mainda-Dvivida in Kishkindha, Parashuram on Mahendra). World-building in 1 line each. |
| 5 | Lineage bridge | 15-20% | Connect the epics through genealogy/teachers: Vyasa = son of Parashar = great-grandson of Vasishtha; Maya danav (who builds the Pandava sabha) = Ravan's father-in-law. The viewer should feel "sab jude hue hain". |
| 6 | Fast-forward chain | 20-35% | Quick chain of famous events (Khandava dahan → Maya sabha → Rajasuya → dice → exile), each with a Ramayana hook. Short sentences, one image per line. |
| 7 | Central encounter (emotional climax) | 35-60% | The thread character meets the new-age hero (Bhima on the Saugandhika path; old monkey whose tail he cannot lift → "Main Hanuman hoon. Bhima, tum mere bhai ho."). Include: humour/humility, a reveal, a teaching (yug theory: dharma on 4→3→2→1 legs), a spectacle (the giant form growing "kelon se upar, chattanon se upar, badalon ke paar — jaise aakash mein doosra suraj"), and a promise for the future (roar merged with Bhima's, seat on Arjuna's dhwaja). |
| 8 | Echoes in the war | 60-92% | Story-within-story (Markandeya tells Yudhishthira the Ram katha — "Rajan, suno…"), then Kurukshetra: Gita shlokas (kapidhwaja 1.20, "Ramah shastrabhritam aham" 10.31), Bhishma-Drona-Karna all taught by Parashuram, Yuyutsu = Vibhishan echo, a dark dharma-dilemma (Satyaki & Bhurishrava). |
| 9 | Closing truth | last 5-8% | Turn the last echo into the thesis. The same words/act meant something else in Treta; in Dwapar they are an excuse. "Wo yug ab nahi raha." End on silence / a single image (Valmiki, Ram on chariot). No "like & subscribe" inside the story — put CTA after, if at all. |

For a **reel (60-90 s)** compress to 5 beats: Hook (1 line) → Image →
Shloka/twist → Encounter or reveal → Closing truth.

## Language rules

- Hindi, Devanagari for VO; spoken, not bookish. Sentences 6-14 words.
  One image or one fact per sentence.
- Mix tatsam words for gravitas (वरदान, युग, धर्म, रणभूमि, ध्वज) with everyday
  Hindi for warmth ("उठा नहीं जाता", "भीम हँसे").
- Respectful forms: श्री राम, हनुमान जी (in VO "हनुमान" is fine), "उन्होंने".
- Rhythm devices: triads ("खेलों से ऊपर, चट्टानों से ऊपर, बादलों के पार"),
  short reveal lines alone on their own line, deliberate pauses marked `(…)`.
- Shlokas: Sanskrit in Devanagari → one-line Hindi meaning → source
  (e.g. वाल्मीकि रामायण, उत्तरकाण्ड / भगवद्गीता 10.31). Max ~1 shloka per 2 minutes.
- Dialogue is sparse and short, attributed clearly ("बूढ़े वानर ने कहा —").
- Never invent shlokas or verse numbers. If unsure, paraphrase and say
  "ऐसा कहा जाता है / लोक-कथा में".

## Visual-cue rules (so the script is production-ready)

The reference uses kathputli / paper-cut 2.5D theatre: a stage curtain
opening, hanging sun and clouds on strings, flat layered sets (ghat, banana
forest, palace arches, Kurukshetra flags), Sanskrit text over-lays, English
caption at bottom. For every VO line give a short `[VISUAL]` cue: set,
characters on stage, one motion (curtain opens, sun lowers on string, figure
grows, arrows spiral). Keep sets reusable — the reference reuses ~10 sets.
If the user also needs the video built, hand the finished script to the
`mythology-25d-kit` skill.

## Self-check before delivering

- [ ] Thread, echo pairs and closing truth are all present and the ending
      pays off the echoes.
- [ ] Every shloka is real, correctly attributed, followed by meaning.
- [ ] Every Dwapar beat has a Treta hook (person, place, lineage or words).
- [ ] Emotional climax has: humility → reveal → teaching → spectacle → promise.
- [ ] Word count matches duration (≈145 Hindi words/min).
- [ ] Folk/regional versions are labelled; no sectarian or disrespectful framing.
- [ ] Each line has a VISUAL cue and an English subtitle.
